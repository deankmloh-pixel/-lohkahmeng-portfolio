The biomedical engineering science — for assessors and lecturers

Every input feature in P21 has a physiological and biomechanical derivation — not a correlation found in data, but a measurable physical quantity with a clinical interpretation. The textbook teaches those derivations. The lab sheet tests them. What follows is the science the system is built on.

🎤 Voice channel — acoustic biomechanics

The larynx is a biological oscillator. Parkinson’s neurodegeneration disrupts basal ganglia control of vocal fold vibration, producing measurable perturbation in cycle-to-cycle frequency (jitter), amplitude (shimmer), and harmonic-to-noise energy (HNR). RPDE and DFA are nonlinear dynamical measures of the vocal system’s departure from periodic self-organisation. Every feature derives from established acoustic biomechanics — not a correlation found in data, but a physical quantity with a physiological interpretation.

✏️ Drawing channel — motor kinematics

The Archimedean spiral and wave tracing tests are established clinical instruments in movement disorder assessment. They quantify Parkinson’s resting tremor (4–6 Hz, measured as lateral amplitude deviation from the guide), bradykinesia (low velocity with high coefficient of variation), and rigidity (reduced stroke smoothness). These are kinematic measurements — displacement, velocity, and acceleration patterns of the upper limb motor system — expressed as biomedical engineering parameters before any classifier sees them.

Learning outcomes — for curriculum mapping

Students completing the textbook and lab sheet will be able to:

🔊Extract and interpret acoustic biomarkers (jitter, shimmer, HNR, RPDE, DFA) from sustained phonation recordings and explain their physiological basis
✏️Analyse spiral and wave drawing tests using motor kinematic parameters and relate findings to PD clinical presentation
🤖Interpret a multi-engine AI consensus board, understand why engines disagree, and apply grey-zone deferral protocol correctly
📊Evaluate machine learning model performance using honest AUC, cross-validation methodology, and class-imbalance considerations
⚕Write a structured clinical referral report from AI engine outputs, with appropriate uncertainty quantification and specialist referral triggers
🏛️Apply PDPA-compliant data governance principles to a real biomedical AI data pipeline

Honest note: AUC range 0.644–0.846 reflects the genuine difficulty of voice-based PD screening with class imbalance (147:48). The SVM and Fuzzy variants are clinical primaries — conservative sensitivity over optimised AUC. The full board consensus is what AEGIS reports; no single engine is used alone.

Grey-zone protocol — a teachable clinical design decision. Any composite score between 35% and 65% triggers automatic specialist referral. AEGIS refuses to call it. This is a deliberate design choice — not a weakness — and it is one of the key learning outcomes in the lab sheet. Students must justify why a grey-zone case should not be auto-classified. That reasoning is the biomedical engineering judgement the course is teaching.

Institutional adoption — publishers, marketers, lecturers

These apps demonstrate clinical AI systems capable of classification, early pre-emptive detection, and progressive augmented management. Because full AEGIS engines and datasets run to several gigabytes, only educational quiz modes are hosted here — the live analysis requires the AEGIS Desktop Application. Institutions wishing to contribute local data for engine refinement are welcome to reach out; we share progressive validation results with every contributing partner and operate in strict accordance with PDPA.

What adopting institutions receive: the complete textbook manuscript · lab sheet with marking rubric and instructor answer set · AEGIS Desktop Application licence · progressive model result updates as the engine board improves · co-attribution in the published edition where applicable.

Dedicated to Professor Dhanjoo N. Ghista. AEGIS traces its theoretical lineage to Prof Ghista’s foundational work in biomedical engineering — his nondimensional index (NDI) framework for physiological system characterisation, and his lifelong conviction that engineering rigour and clinical compassion are the same discipline approached from two directions. The grey-zone protocol in P21 — the decision to refuse a call rather than force a verdict the system cannot honestly support — is the computational expression of that humility. AEGIS augments, it never replaces.
